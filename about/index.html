My Hobbies

In recent years, I've started to fall back to a lot of the hobbies I had when I was a kid. Namely drawing, playing guitar, and occasional programming (admittedly probably not as much as I should). However, I have picked up some new hobbies, like learning to use a Linux desktop, home-labbing, and making videos on YouTube.

I've been spending a lot of my time learning how to draw "correctly" by learning fundamentals and really understanding the form of the things I draw. When I was a kid, I never really tried drawing from imagination, nor learning how objects actually existed in 3D space. Most of the time, my drawing would just consist of copying down a frame of an anime that I liked, or following drawing tutorials on DragoArt. Thankfully, after many years of drawing, I finally feel like I can consistently draw what I'm trying to draw, without just copying the contours of the figure I'm trying to draw. Thinking in 3D really helps you to understand why others draw certain lines and shapes when making their own drawings. It's really opened my eyes to the importance of observation when making art. If you can't properly break down what you're observing into easily understandable shapes, it's easy to get lost in details.

Guitar is something that I've practiced since I was around 7 years old or so. I mostly got into it because some of my family were really into rock, and started learning the guitar to play their favorite songs. At some point I decided it'd look cool to play the guitar, so my Uncle bought me my first Ibanez electric guitar. Similarly to my approach to art, I didn't really take time to study and learn fundamentals, I just wanted to cover songs that already exist. I'm still kind of like that today. I've learned chords and progressions mostly just from raw mileage from playing a bunch of songs from various artists. I wouldn't consider myself amazing at guitar now, but I can learn how to comfortably play a song that's not too complicated in a half hour or so. I have posted guitar covers in the past, but it's not really something I do anymore. That being said, I have made a few recordings of covers that I may upload at some point after I workshop them a little bit.

Programming is both my hobby, and the career I've decided to pursue. If I'm being totally honest, I sort of fell into this field without too much thought. In high school I took a couple of programming classes to get out of taking a foreign language class (I was lazy), and had a pretty easy time with it. At the time, programming was extremely lucrative and highly sought after. I remember being told multiple times throughout high school and college that "If you're a programmer, you're set for life. Pretty much every company is going to need programmers." Because of this, I decided to go all in on programming when I went to college. It took me an extra year, but I graduated with a BS in Computer Engineering. Since graduating, I've spent some time at my job learning C#, SQL, and WinForms. On my own time, I've learned bits and pieces of the DevOps cycle, designed and created a RESTful API, and revisited my web design skills (that's what I'm doing now!). I've also learned that not keeping up with programming and business trends will cause you to be left behind, as in some ways I feel like I'm having to totally relearn programming after taking the past year or so off. But, I'm slowly regaining my confidence in development, and hope to work on some bigger projects soon!

Linux has been my main hobby for the past couple years now. There's something that I genuinely find addicting about the amount of choice and customization that Linux offers. I recently have gotten into the trend of "ricing" and is how I spend a lot of my time when at my PC. My current desktop of choice at the moment is awesomewm, though I have dabbled in a few other environments like KDE Plasma, Hyprland, and i3wm. At the moment, I'm using this cool foresty green theme that I designed myself using various awesome widgets and looking through A LOT of dotfiles.
